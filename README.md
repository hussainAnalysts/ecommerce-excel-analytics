# Maven Fuzzy Factory — E-commerce Analytics

## Background & Overview

Maven Fuzzy Factory is an e-commerce business operating across multiple digital touchpoints, generating data from website sessions, pageviews, orders, products, and refunds.

This project was developed to evaluate the company's commercial performance from an end-to-end perspective, connecting website traffic with conversion, orders, revenue, profitability, marketing acquisition, product performance, and device behaviour.

The analysis was designed around a fundamental business question:

> **Is the business generating sustainable commercial value from the traffic it attracts?**

Rather than evaluating revenue or website traffic independently, the analysis connects the full customer journey:

**Website Sessions → Conversion → Orders → Revenue → Profitability**

This provides a more comprehensive view of business performance and highlights where growth is being driven by genuine efficiency versus increased traffic volume.

# Executive Summary

The analysis indicates that Maven Fuzzy Factory experienced a significant change in commercial performance throughout the period.
<img width="1642" height="648" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/116d2785-a412-409f-a0b7-5f1f0f92f91c" />

Revenue declined sharply during the middle of the year, falling from approximately **$214.4K in February to $108.2K in April**. However, the business subsequently recovered, reaching approximately **$219.9K in December**, its highest monthly revenue during the analysed period.

Website traffic followed a similar pattern. Sessions declined from approximately **47.2K in February to 29.1K in April**, before recovering strongly to **55.5K sessions in December**.

The recovery in traffic, however, was not matched by an equivalent recovery in conversion efficiency. The session-to-order conversion rate peaked at **7.59% in February**, declined to **6.51% in April**, and ended at **6.96% in December**.

This distinction is important. December generated substantially more traffic than February, but conversion efficiency and revenue per session remained below their earlier levels. This indicates that the recovery was driven largely by increased traffic volume rather than a complete recovery in traffic monetisation.

Marketing performance was highly concentrated around **Google Search**, which generated approximately **$1.22M in revenue** and **21.3K orders**.

Device performance was similarly concentrated, with **Desktop contributing approximately 86% of total revenue**.

Overall, the analysis suggests that the next stage of growth should focus not simply on acquiring more visitors, but on improving **traffic quality, conversion efficiency, and the value generated from each website session**.

# Insights Deep Dive

## Revenue Performance

Revenue experienced a substantial contraction during the middle of the year.

Monthly revenue declined from approximately **$214.4K in February to $108.2K in April**, representing an approximate **49% reduction**.

The business subsequently recovered, reaching approximately **$219.9K in December**.

The recovery is encouraging, but the scale of the earlier decline suggests that management should investigate the underlying drivers rather than treating the recovery as an isolated improvement.

Possible areas for further investigation include changes in website traffic, marketing activity, conversion behaviour, product demand, and customer purchasing patterns.

---

## Website Traffic and Conversion
<img width="1198" height="623" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b4d43596-8ae0-4f5c-9128-cd02cd096fe0" />


Website sessions declined from approximately **47.2K in February to 29.1K in April**, before recovering to **55.5K in December**.

At first glance, the December traffic figure appears highly positive because it represents the highest monthly session volume observed.

However, conversion tells a different story.

The session-to-order conversion rate reached **7.59% in February**, compared with **6.96% in December**.

Therefore, December attracted more visitors but converted a smaller proportion of those visitors into orders.

This highlights a critical e-commerce principle:

> **Traffic growth does not automatically translate into conversion growth.**

For management, the focus should therefore shift from simply increasing sessions toward understanding the **quality and commercial value of those sessions**.

---

## Order per Session

Order per session provides an additional perspective on traffic efficiency.

Order per session was approximately:

- **4.54k in February**
- **3.72k in April**
- **3.96k in December**

The metric declined significantly during the mid-year downturn and recovered only partially by December.

This means the business was generating more traffic by year-end, but each individual session was still producing less revenue than during the February peak.

The implication is significant:

> **The business recovered traffic faster than it recovered traffic monetisation.**

Future growth initiatives should therefore evaluate both **session volume and revenue per session**.

The analysis shows a significant concentration of revenue by device.

Desktop accounted for approximately **86% of total revenue**, while Mobile contributed approximately **14%**.

If mobile represents a meaningful proportion of website traffic but contributes only a small proportion of revenue, the business should investigate potential friction in the mobile customer journey, particularly around product discovery and checkout.

# Key Findings

The analysis can be summarised into five major findings:

**1. Revenue recovered strongly after a significant mid-year decline.**

The business moved from approximately **$108.2K in April to $219.9K in December**, demonstrating a strong recovery in commercial activity.

**2. Traffic recovered faster than conversion efficiency.**

December recorded the highest session volume, but its **6.96% conversion rate remained below February's 7.59% peak**.

**3. Traffic monetisation has not fully recovered.**

December revenue per session was approximately **$3.96**, below the February level of approximately **$4.54**.

**4. Google Search is the dominant acquisition channel.**

The channel generated approximately **$1.22M in revenue**, making it the largest commercial acquisition driver in the analysis.

**5. Revenue is heavily concentrated on Desktop.**

Desktop contributed approximately **86% of revenue**, creating an opportunity for deeper mobile performance analysis.

# Recommendations

## Improve Conversion Efficiency

Management should investigate the drivers behind the decline in conversion from the February peak.

The analysis should extend into the customer funnel to identify potential friction across landing pages, product pages, cart activity, and checkout.

## Focus on Traffic Quality, Not Just Traffic Volume

The business should track **Revenue per Session** alongside website sessions.

This will help determine whether additional traffic is creating proportional commercial value.

## Optimise Marketing Investment

Google Search should remain an important acquisition channel, but future investment decisions should incorporate conversion, profitability, and acquisition costs rather than relying solely on revenue contribution.

## Investigate Mobile Customer Experience

Given the substantial revenue concentration on Desktop, management should assess mobile conversion performance and identify potential friction within the mobile purchasing journey.

## Leverage Customer Demand Patterns

The significant variation in order activity by day of week can be incorporated into campaign scheduling, promotional planning, inventory management, and operational resource allocation.

## Strengthen Product-Level Profitability Analysis

Product performance should be evaluated using revenue, units sold, gross profit, margin, and refunds.

This will help identify products that generate genuine economic value rather than simply high sales volume.

# Analytical Approach

The project followed a structured analytical workflow:

**Data Extraction → Data Cleaning → Data Transformation → Data Modelling → DAX KPI Development → Exploratory Analysis → Dashboard Development → Business Insights → Recommendations**

Power Query was used to prepare and transform the datasets, while Power Pivot and DAX were used to create the analytical model and calculate business KPIs.

The final dashboard converts the underlying data into a management-oriented reporting layer that enables stakeholders to evaluate performance across time, marketing channels, products, and devices.

# Caveats & Assumptions

The analysis is primarily descriptive and diagnostic. The identified relationships should therefore not automatically be interpreted as causal.

Marketing channel performance is based on the available website attribution fields and should be supplemented with marketing spend data for a complete ROI assessment.

Similarly, device-level revenue concentration does not by itself establish that one device performs better than another. Device traffic, conversion, revenue per session, and customer journey behaviour should be assessed together.

Revenue and profitability calculations are also dependent on the treatment of costs and refunds within the underlying datasets.

---

# Conclusion

The Maven Fuzzy Factory analysis demonstrates that strong e-commerce performance management requires more than monitoring revenue and website traffic independently.

The more meaningful question is:

> **How efficiently is the business converting digital activity into sustainable economic value?**

By connecting **Traffic → Conversion → Orders → Revenue → Profitability**, the dashboard provides a more complete view of business performance and highlights opportunities to improve conversion efficiency, traffic monetisation, marketing effectiveness, mobile performance, and product profitability.

The project demonstrates how Excel, Power Query, Power Pivot, and DAX can be combined to transform operational data into **decision-ready commercial intelligence**.
